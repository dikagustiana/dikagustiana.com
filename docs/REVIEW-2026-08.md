## 1. Verdict

If a scholarship panelist or DFI recruiter opened this site tomorrow, they would meet a
well-built, fast, honest-looking publishing system — and then the two things that actually decide
their opinion would both wobble. First: the site's dominant visible fact is what doesn't exist
yet. The Finance section's flagship track greets them with "0 of 56 essays published" over twenty
rows of zeros, five of seven top-level sections contain no essays at all, and the roadmap's
ambition reads as an empty building because absence is presented in the same visual grammar, and
at larger volume, than work. Second, and worse because it poisons the strongest evidence: **the
essays a serious reader would actually read still contain visible drafting scaffolding** — a
citation to an "attachment PDF", a reference to a nonexistent "Artifact C", the sentence "This
deliberately narrows the previous draft's stricter sequencing claim", a value missing mid-sentence
("has fallen from its late-1990s peak to."), and a formula that renders as `deltaC0` — most of it
concentrated in the one essay an Imperial climate-finance reader is most likely to open. The
thinking in those essays is genuinely strong; the arithmetic checks out to the decimal; the
citations carry page numbers. That is precisely why the leaked scaffolding is so expensive: it
converts "this person thinks rigorously" into "this person ships unread drafts", which for a
writing-based application is the worst available trade. Meanwhile the backup that was designed
after this site already lost its entire corpus once has failed all five of its scheduled runs and
nobody has noticed, and the bare domain `dikagustiana.com` — the address on every canonical tag —
silently redirects readers to a months-old abandoned deployment. None of this is structural. The
machine is good. The five fixes at the top of this list are mostly editing and configuration, and
they close the gap between what the system deserves and what a stranger concludes.

## 2. Top 5

Ranked by damage to the audience. The brief says the curriculum gap is the single most important
thing; after reading every published essay, I am deliberately placing the scaffolding leaks above
it, and saying why: the empty curriculum reads as *ambition not yet delivered* — survivable,
even sympathetic. Leaked revision notes inside the published essays read as *the author didn't
read their own published work*, which attacks the one thing the site exists to prove. The reader
who never scrolls past the homepage sees the empty building; the reader you most need to convince
— the one who actually reads an essay — hits the scaffolding.

### 1. Scrub the five published essays of drafting scaffolding (F01, P0 — effort S)

Nine distinct artifacts across three of five essays, worst in *Indonesia's Reindustrialization
Bet* — the single most relevant essay for an Imperial/Chevening reader. Every one is listed with
its exact string in WO-F01; the whole fix is an hour of editing. This beats the curriculum fix
because it is the cheapest P0, and because it protects the asset every other fix is trying to
showcase.

### 2. Set the two backup secrets and prove one restore (F03, P0 — effort S)

The daily backup workflow has **failed on schedule five times out of five** (2026-08-02 →
2026-08-07, fresh from the Actions API) because `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and
`SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` were never set as repository secrets. This site has already lost its
entire authored corpus once — the published Site Rebuild Note says so in its own words. The
at-risk set is now precisely known: 5 essay bodies (~76k chars), 48 revisions, module metadata.
Fifteen minutes of configuration removes the only failure mode that is unrecoverable. It ranks
above the curriculum because it is the only item that can destroy work rather than discount it.

### 3. Make the published corpus, not the absence, the first thing a reader meets (F02, P0 — effort M)

Keep all 49 modules and the honest counts. Change what carries the weight: today
`/finance/fundamentals` says "0 of 56 essays published" and then twenty rows of "0 of N
published" (screenshot evidence; `src/pages/FinanceTrackIndex.tsx:285-295, 133-137`), and one
expanded module at 375px fills two screens with "Coming soon · Dika Gustiana" rows. The
recommendation and the rejected alternative are in WO-F02; the register of the fix is
"published-first with a deliberate roadmap view", not hiding the structure and not deleting it.

### 4. Give the About page an author (F04, P0 — effort S)

`/about` currently contains one italic line, Selected Essays, and a reading list
(`src/pages/About.tsx:107-175`). No name, no role, no CFA, no prior record, no contact, no
views-are-my-own boundary. The only identity on the site lives in the footer
(`src/components/Footer.tsx:22-29`). A panel reads the author before it credits the argument;
right now the site withholds its best evidence. Owner supplies wording; structure is in WO-F04.

### 5. Point the bare domain at the real site (F05, P0 — effort S)

`https://dikagustiana.com/` (and every path under it) returns **308 →
`https://dika-s-digital-studio.vercel.app/`** — an old deployment serving a different, older
bundle (`/assets/index-tLuHZmHp.js`) with generic meta tags and no per-essay prerender. Every
`<link rel="canonical">` and `og:url` on the current site targets the apex
(`scripts/prerender.mjs:23`), so search engines and anyone who types the naked domain are being
handed the stale site. This is a Vercel domain-assignment fix plus a redeploy — but it must be
done in the Vercel dashboard, which this session cannot reach (the connected Vercel account shows
no projects).

The sixth item — the publish→distribution pipeline (F07) — loses to these five only because each
of them changes what a reader concludes this week; F07 changes how reliably future publishes
reach readers.
